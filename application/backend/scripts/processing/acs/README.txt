The **American Community Survey (ACS)** is an ongoing, nationwide survey conducted by the **U.S. Census Bureau** to provide detailed, up-to-date information about the social, economic, housing, and demographic characteristics of the U.S. population. Unlike the decennial census, which aims to count every resident once every ten years, the ACS is administered continuously to a sample of households each year. This design allows the Census Bureau to produce timely estimates that reflect changes in communities over time, making the ACS a core data source for understanding how populations and living conditions evolve between censuses.

The ACS collects a wide range of data, including information on age, sex, race and ethnicity, education, income, employment, commuting patterns, health insurance coverage, housing characteristics, and housing costs. These data are released as one-year and five-year estimates, with the latter providing greater statistical reliability for smaller geographic areas such as census tracts, towns, and rural communities. Governments, researchers, businesses, and nonprofits use ACS data for purposes ranging from allocating federal funds and planning infrastructure to conducting academic research and informing policy decisions, making it one of the most important and widely used statistical products in the United States.
